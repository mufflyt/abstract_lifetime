Missingness was assessed for 10 analytic variables across 1,106 units, separated into item-level (n=10) and structural, missing-by-design (n=0) mechanisms. Item-level missingness ranged from 2.2% to 83.8% (greatest for subspecialty) and reflects source non-linkage that is independent of the subgroup structure, rather than missingness conditioned on a unit's subgroup or observation status. Little's MCAR test on the numeric item-level block (4 variables) gave chi-square = 28.8, df = 7, p = 0.000154; we therefore reject the null of MCAR. At this sample size (N=1,106) Little's test is highly powered and rejects MCAR on minor deviations; rejection here is consistent with weak, non-systematic item missingness rather than evidence of substantial bias, and complete-case analyses of these variables are unlikely to be materially confounded.
